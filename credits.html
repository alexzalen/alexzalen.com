Credits

  • Brain surface. FreeSurfer fsaverage template (Martinos Center for Biomedical Imaging, Massachusetts General Hospital), used under the FreeSurfer Software License Agreement, Part B: licence text. This mesh is a modified version: it uses the 10,242-vertex-per-hemisphere fsaverage5 subsampling, coordinates are quantised to 16-bit integers, and the sulcal-depth values (from the FreeSurfer sulc maps) are rescaled to 8 bits. Triangle connectivity comes from the fsaverage5 files shipped with the nilearn package. MGH and the FreeSurfer contributors do not endorse this site.
  • Real-brain glitch. The brief photorealistic frames show the same FreeSurfer fsaverage surface at the finer fsaverage6 resolution (40,962 vertices per hemisphere), under the same licence. This mesh is a modified version too: 16-bit coordinates in the same frame as the ASCII brain, sulcal depth rescaled to 8 bits, and two values per vertex computed for this site (ambient occlusion from a voxelised copy of the surface, and mean curvature) (brain_hd.bin.gz). Triangle connectivity comes from the fsaverage6 files shipped with nilearn. The tissue colours, surface texture and blood vessels are made up by the renderer, not taken from a photograph.
  • Parahippocampal place area. The PPA vertices come from the scene-selective group parcels of Julian, J. B., Fedorenko, E., Webster, J., & Kanwisher, N. (2012). An algorithmic method for functionally defining regions of interest in the ventral visual pathway. NeuroImage, 60(4), 2357–2364. doi:10.1016/j.neuroimage.2012.02.055
  • Amygdala. The amygdala outline is adapted from the Harvard-Oxford subcortical structural atlas: segmentations by the Harvard Center for Morphometric Analysis; Makris et al. (2006), Schizophrenia Research 83:155–171; Frazier et al. (2005), American Journal of Psychiatry 162:1256–1265; Desikan et al. (2006), NeuroImage 31:968–980; Goldstein et al. (2007), Biological Psychiatry 61:935–945. The atlas is packaged with FSL (FMRIB, University of Oxford; atlas page) and, according to the FSL licence page (checked 17 November 2026), is released under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International licence (CC BY-SA 4.0). Changes: the left and right amygdala of the 2 mm maximum-probability map (25% threshold) were turned into a coarse surface (marching cubes, light smoothing, coordinates rounded to 0.1 mm). This simplified shape (amygdala.json) is shared under the same licence, CC BY-SA 4.0; the rest of this site is not covered by it. Not for measurement. The atlas creators, the Harvard Center for Morphometric Analysis, FMRIB and the University of Oxford do not endorse this site.
  • Brain areas. The lines on what each brain area does are simplified summaries written for this site, and the dots mark the places only approximately.
  • Speaker numbers. The numbers next to the language codes are estimated, live; modelled, not counted. They start from the Ethnologue 2026 totals (first plus second language) and change with a random model of gains and losses based on UN population data. How they are made.
  • Arabic and Urdu font. Arabic and Urdu text is set in Kawkab Mono by Abdullah Arif, used under the SIL Open Font License 1.1 (licence text), cut down to the letters used here and scaled to the site’s character grid. Urdu is therefore shown in a Naskh style, not in the usual Nastaliq. Hindi, Bengali and Chinese use the fonts of your system, with each character placed on the same grid.
  • Digit font. The speaker numbers in the language list are set in Roboto Mono (The Roboto Mono Project Authors), used under the SIL Open Font License 1.1 (licence text), cut down to the digits 0–9. Each digit is as heavy and as bright as its value.
  • Tools. Mesh files were handled with nibabel, NumPy and SciPy, and nilearn / MNE-derived fsaverage data. (To be confirmed: exact provenance of the intermediate fsaverage files.)
